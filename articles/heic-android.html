How to Open HEIC Photos on Android

Someone sent you iPhone photos and they won't open? Here's the fix · 4 min read

A friend with an iPhone sends you photos, and your Android phone shows a gray thumbnail or "can't open file". The photos aren't broken — they're in HEIC, Apple's space-saving format that many Android gallery apps still refuse to display. Three ways to fix it, fastest first.

Fix 1: Convert in the browser (works on any Android)

Open myheicgo.com in Chrome on the phone, tap the drop zone to pick the HEIC files from your Downloads or WhatsApp folder, and they convert to JPG right on the device. No app install, no upload to any server — the conversion happens inside the browser itself, which matters when the photos are personal.

Fix 2: The WhatsApp trick (zero extra steps)

Ask the sender to send the photos through WhatsApp instead of email or AirDrop. WhatsApp automatically compresses images to JPG before sending — the Android side opens them instantly. The trade-off is reduced resolution (fine for viewing, not for printing).

Fix 3: Ask the sender to switch formats

For ongoing photo sharing, the iPhone user can flip one setting: Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible. New photos will be JPG from then on. We explain the trade-offs of that switch in this guide — in short: bigger files on their phone, zero problems on yours.

Why does this happen at all?

Since 2017, iPhones default to HEIC because it stores the same visual quality in roughly half the space of JPG. Google's newer Android phones and many galleries have added HEIC support, but the Android world is enormous and fragmented — plenty of phones, file managers and apps still don't recognize it. Conversion to JPG remains the universal bridge; our full explainer on HEIC vs JPG covers the details.

Quick recap

  • One-off photos: convert them yourself in the browser — 30 seconds, no install
  • Viewing only: ask for WhatsApp delivery
  • Long-term sharing: sender switches camera format
