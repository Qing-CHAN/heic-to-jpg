How to Make Your iPhone Shoot JPG Instead of HEIC

Exact steps, plus why you might not want to · 4 min read

Tired of photos that won't open on other devices? You can tell your iPhone to save photos as JPG from now on. It takes fifteen seconds. Whether you should is a different question — we'll cover both.

The exact steps

  1. Open Settings on your iPhone.
  2. Scroll down and tap Camera.
  3. Tap Formats.
  4. Select Most Compatible instead of High Efficiency.

That's it. From this point, every new photo is saved as JPG. Photos you took before the change remain HEIC — convert existing files with our free browser converter.

What you gain

  • Photos open on every Windows PC, Android phone and website without conversion
  • No surprises when uploading to portals that only accept JPG/PNG
  • Files plug straight into older editing software

What you give up

  • Roughly double the storage per photo — for a 256GB phone storing 20,000 photos, that is tens of gigabytes
  • Slightly less detail in tricky lighting at the same file size
  • Loss of HEIC-only niceties like efficient bursts and depth maps in one container

The smarter middle path

Keep the phone on High Efficiency (HEIC) and convert only when a situation demands it. Typical flow: shoot in HEIC all year, and when you need to upload documents to a portal or send photos to a Windows user, run them through our HEIC to JPG converter — it works locally in your browser, handles whole albums at once, and never uploads your photos anywhere.

For screenshots and graphics destined for design tools or transparent backgrounds, use our HEIC to PNG converter instead — PNG is lossless.

Bonus: fix transfers without changing formats

If your only problem is cable transfers to a PC, there's a targeted fix: Settings → Photos → Transfer to Mac or PC → Automatic. The iPhone then hands Windows a JPG copy while your library stays in space-saving HEIC. See our full guide to opening HEIC on Windows for all four methods compared.
