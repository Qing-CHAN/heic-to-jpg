How to Batch Convert HEIC Photos to JPG

For whole albums, archives and exports · 4 min read

Converting one photo is easy. The real problem is the folder with 400 vacation photos that won't open on your PC, or the iCloud export your website refuses. Here is how to convert large batches to JPG — without paying for software or uploading your entire photo library to a stranger's server.

The free way: browser batch conversion

Our HEIC to JPG converter processes as many files as you drop into it:

  1. Select everything — in File Explorer, press Ctrl+A inside your HEIC folder, then drag the whole selection onto the drop zone.
  2. Wait for the queue — files convert one by one in the background. The status under each file shows original size → converted size.
  3. Download all as ZIP — one click packages every converted JPG into a single archive.

Because conversion happens locally in your browser (WebAssembly), a folder of 500 photos never leaves your machine. There is no upload wait, no file size cap, and no privacy risk — even sensitive personal photos stay on your device.

Performance tip: conversion speed depends on your CPU. Closing heavy browser tabs frees memory for very large batches (1000+ files). The queue keeps every original intact — the converted copies land in a ZIP, originals never move.

When something fails

A file that reports "failed to convert" is usually one of three things: a corrupted download from iCloud, a renamed file (a JPG wearing a .heic name), or an out-of-date browser. Update your browser and retry the failed file alone — the rest of the batch is unaffected either way.

Alternatives compared

Preview on Mac: select all → Open → Export. Free and fast, but Mac-only.

Command line tools (ImageMagick, libheif): powerful and scriptable, but intimidating if you don't live in a terminal.

Paid converter apps: the Microsoft Store is full of $5–20 "HEIC converters" that do exactly what free browser tools do. Skip them.

What about PNG output?

If your destination needs PNG — design tools, docs with transparency, maximum quality — the same converter offers a HEIC to PNG mode. Expect much larger files: PNG is lossless by design.
