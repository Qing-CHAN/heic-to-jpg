How to Open iPhone HEIC Photos on Windows

Four working methods, ranked by convenience · 5 min read

You copied photos from an iPhone to a Windows PC, double-clicked, and got an error — or a file that refuses to preview. That's because your iPhone saves photos in HEIC format, which Windows only opens in certain configurations. Here are four ways to fix it, from fastest to most permanent.

Method 1: Convert in your browser (fastest, no install)

The quickest route is an online converter that works locally — like our HEIC to JPG converter. Drag the files in, and they are converted to JPG right on your computer using WebAssembly. Nothing is uploaded, so it is safe for personal photos, and it works even on locked-down office PCs where you cannot install anything.

  • ✅ Nothing to install, works in any modern browser
  • ✅ Handles many files at once (download all as one ZIP)
  • ✅ Private — files never leave your machine

Method 2: Install the free HEIF extensions from Microsoft

Open the Microsoft Store and install "HEIF Image Extensions" (free). Many PCs also need "HEVC Video Extensions", which historically cost $0.99 but is often bundled on new machines. After installing, the built-in Photos app will preview HEIC files directly.

  • ✅ One-time setup, then HEIC previews like any photo
  • ❌ Doesn't help with websites, old apps or uploading — those still need JPG
  • ❌ The HEVC extension isn't free on all systems

Method 3: Change how the iPhone transfers photos

When connecting by cable, on the iPhone go to Settings → Photos → Transfer to Mac or PC and choose Automatic. Windows then receives a JPG copy instead of the HEIC original. This fixes future transfers without touching your camera settings.

  • ✅ Free, built in, no quality loss beyond normal JPG compression
  • ❌ Only helps with cable transfers — AirDrop, iCloud and manual copies still deliver HEIC
  • ❌ Transfers become slower (the phone converts on the fly)

Method 4: Shoot JPG from now on

Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible makes the iPhone save JPG natively. This permanently solves the problem but doubles photo storage usage and disables some HEIC features. Most photographers prefer keeping HEIC and converting when sharing — see our guide on iPhone camera formats for the trade-offs.

Which should you pick?

For most people: Method 1 for the photos you already have, plus Method 3 so the next batch arrives as JPG automatically. That combination requires zero installs and covers both past and future photos. And if a website demands PNG instead of JPG, our HEIC to PNG converter handles that too.
